## Huddle
A short, focused team meeting to agree on the next work, responsibilities and open questions.
Example: Spend at least 10 minutes comparing preparation before choosing your production plan.

## Huddle board
The shared visual record of a huddle: a sketch, decisions, owners, disagreements and open questions.
Example: A pipeline diagram with sticky notes explaining why the team chose its player controller.

## Attention Accord
The team’s agreement about purpose, focus, capture methods, promised evidence and how the team will use what members bring back.
Example: Each member names a question, an artifact they will return with, and how it helps the mission.

## Attention budget
The limited time and concentration available for the work. Plan where to spend it.
Example: Give setup 15 minutes and testing 20; leave room to document failures.

## ADD
Attention-Driven Design: in this course, plan how people direct attention, understand choices and act on evidence.
Example: Use one clear task, visible progress and an understandable handoff instead of competing instructions.

## P2P
Pathways to Pipelines: in this course, turn a chosen route into a connected process with usable evidence.
Example: Move from story intent to organized source, engine setup, a tested player and a handoff.

## Orchestrator mindset
Choose a purpose, coordinate people and tools, assign responsibility and verify how the pieces work together.
Example: Choose who will inspect the import, who will document it and how both results will be tested.

## Pitch
A short, specific explanation of what you understand and can contribute to a mission.
Example: Share your contribution form and explain the useful work you can take responsibility for.

## Mission
The concrete result your team or solo project is trying to achieve.
Example: Make a small scene in which the story’s courier can move through an imported environment.

## Pipeline
Connected stages that turn inputs into an output, with checks between stages.
Example: Goal → assets → import → configure → run → test → document.

## Task owner
The person responsible for completing and recording a task. Others may help.
Example: Jordan owns the player import and records the files and test result.

## Milestone
A meaningful checkpoint showing that a defined part of the work is ready.
Example: The project opens on another team member’s computer.

## Dependency
Work or information that another task needs before it can proceed.
Example: Testing the controller depends on importing and configuring it first.

## Deliverable
A usable item someone can open, inspect or build on.
Example: An engine project, numbered PDF, readable board image or tested source commit.

## Acceptance criteria
Observable conditions used to decide whether the work meets its purpose.
Example: The project opens, the player is present, movement is tested and instructions identify the relevant script.

## Scope
The work included in this assignment or iteration.
Example: Finish the pipeline through Video 6; extra effects do not replace the required evidence.

## Handoff
The information and files another person needs to continue, inspect or reproduce the work.
Example: A personal repository link with source, tested instructions, boards and issued PDFs.

## Blocker
A specific problem preventing the next task from proceeding.
Example: An import error stops the scene from opening; capture the error and the attempted fix.

## Retrospective
A brief review of what happened, what the evidence supports and what to change next.
Example: Record one frustration, its cause or uncertainty, and the next test.

## PREP code
A reference to an issued personal preparation record. It verifies the stored form, not that the learner watched or understood everything.
Example: Share the current PREP code with a prospective lead.

## TIME code
A reference to the learner’s daily check-in and reviewed late result.
Example: A lead checks the Action A TIME code before inviting the learner.

## Team form
The issued record of accepted members and the current agreed plan. Solo uses a one-person form.
Example: Reissue the team form after changing membership or the plan.

## Package
A supplied collection of code and/or assets that can be added to a project.
Example: Inspect a player package before importing and configuring its controller.

## using directive
C# syntax that makes namespace types available by name. It does not install a package or run the imported code.
Example: using UnityEngine; lets a script refer to MonoBehaviour and other UnityEngine types.
